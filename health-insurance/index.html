Start here: what do these terms mean?
Premium
Payment for coverage, even when you receive no care. Annual premiums = monthly premium × 12.
Deductible
Amount you pay toward services subject to the deductible before the plan begins sharing those costs.
Copay
A fixed payment for a covered service. Whether the deductible applies depends on the plan.
Coinsurance
Your percentage of eligible costs after the applicable deductible. A 20% patient share means the plan pays the other 80%, until the maximum changes the split.
Allowed amount
The amount recognized by the plan for a service. This can differ from the provider's billed price.
Out-of-pocket maximum
A limit on eligible spending for covered, in-network care in the plan year. Premiums are separate.

Rules used in this comparison

Both plans use fixed office-visit copays before the deductible. These copays count toward the out-of-pocket maximum, but do not reduce the deductible. Other medical expenses use the deductible, then patient coinsurance. Office visits are processed first, followed by the other medical expenses.

Office visits represent the visit copay only; additional tests or procedures belong in other medical expenses. The same allowed amounts apply to both plans. Actual plans can have different visit rules, allowed amounts, coverage, networks, or separate deductibles.

Model limits and research sources

This is a simplified teaching model, not a quote for a real plan. It excludes family coverage, out-of-network and noncovered care, separate drug deductibles, subsidies, employer contributions, and tax effects. Certain preventive services may be covered without cost sharing; they are not included in these charged visits.

The annual comparison begins with no deductible or eligible spending paid and 12 months of coverage. The single-bill view lets you explore spending already paid. The out-of-pocket limit does not include premiums or noncovered expenses. Provider access, benefits, and affordability matter alongside modeled cost.
